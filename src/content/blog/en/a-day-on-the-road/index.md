What does a digital nomad's day look like?

A beach? A laptop by the pool? A new city every morning?

Mine doesn't look like that. Most of my days are ordinary days. They just happen somewhere else.

## Morning

I've always been an early riser, and I've always started work early. I rarely stay up late. Early to bed, early to rise.

I used to start the day at home: make breakfast, catch up on the news, then dive into work. These days I go out more. As soon as the cafés open, I settle into one and get to work. Working from home all the time kills my motivation, so I think that's why I like it this way. I wrote about how I find these cafés in [Cafés Are My Office](/blog/cafes-are-my-office/).

## Work

I usually work more than 10 hours a day. Sometimes I burn out, and that number drops a lot. Everything has its seasons.

Clients' time zones shape my day a lot. If I'm working with someone, I have to adapt to their hours. So when I choose a country, I try to pick one where I won't end up working at night. And if it's the other way round, a place where I can get up very early and work, I pick it without a second thought.

## Afternoon

I walk. Without headphones, just me and my thoughts.

Sometimes it's the neighbourhood. Sometimes a part of the city I haven't seen yet. I wrote about that in [my post on getting to know a city on foot](/blog/getting-to-know-a-city-on-foot/).

## Evening

Some evenings I eat out. Some I cook.

And sometimes I just drink my coffee and watch the sunset. I don't need to be doing something every moment.

## Before the day ends

I record what I spent. Always the same day; it's a habit now. The rate is right, and no coffee gets forgotten. I explained why the day matters in [my post on which exchange rate to record expenses at](/blog/which-exchange-rate-to-record-expenses/).

## Not every day is like this

I know bad things can always happen, and I try to be mentally ready for them. If bad things never happened, I don't think we'd notice the good ones, or value them.

Health feels like the most important thing. Sometimes a headache alone is enough to ruin the day. I can't even imagine bigger health problems.

If the internet is down or something goes wrong with the flat, I don't worry much. I go straight to the alternatives. No internet? I go out to wander and explore. Need to change flats? I start looking right away. Letting small things like these ruin my mood seems silly to me.

## Days for myself

For me, weekends are the days I keep for myself, and they don't have to fall on a Saturday or Sunday. On those days I try not to think about work too much and just live. I focus on whatever lets me enjoy life: short trips, meeting friends, trying something new.

In fact, since I know weekends get crowded, I often work through them and save these things for quieter days. That just makes more sense to me.

From the outside, a nomad's life looks like travel. From the inside, most of it is simply life: work, a walk, dinner, a bit of quiet. In a place that's new for a while.

I wish you good ordinary days, wherever you are.
