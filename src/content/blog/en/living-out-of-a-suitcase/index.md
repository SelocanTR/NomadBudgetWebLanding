Could your life on the road fit in one suitcase?

Mine does.

It didn't start that way. My first long trip was a 65-litre backpack. I'd researched it so much, I could have called myself a backpacking expert.

Years later I wrote [No More Backpacking, I Love My Wheeled Suitcase](https://medium.com/millennials/no-more-backpacking-i-love-my-wheeled-suitcase-6df6d435e40b). A short trip and a long stay are very different things. One, three or six months don't fit in a backpack.

## Why a suitcase

**It's not a big one.** I always keep it within the limits.

**Things stay organised.** Everything has a place. You don't dig to the bottom of a bag to find one shirt.

**It's not on your shoulders.** It rolls.

**Rain? Dirt?** It's just plastic. Wipe it and go.

## Why I never buy an expensive one

I don't buy expensive suitcases.

I let it break. Then I buy a new one, and move on.

A suitcase on the road gets thrown, dragged, rained on and squeezed into luggage racks. An expensive one hurts every time it gets a scratch. A cheap one just does its job until it doesn't.

## What's inside

Like every digital nomad, I can't go without my electronics. Laptop, power bank, headphones: they don't go in the suitcase. They always live in the small backpack I keep with me.

As I mentioned in [A Kitchen on the Road](/blog/a-kitchen-on-the-road/), I also carry things most people would find odd, like spices, a mug and a teapot. And to be ready for any weather, I always pack a raincoat and a hat.

I'm fairly minimalist, really. All my clothes go with each other, so I can wear them in different combinations.

For changing seasons, I usually pack things I can layer: light, medium and warm. The warmer the layer, the fewer I take, naturally. I don't take many of anything, so when something wears out, I just buy a replacement locally.

## What I stopped carrying

Some things don't need a suitcase at all. My digital subscriptions travel with me; I can use them anywhere.

I used to carry accessories like a laptop stand and a mouse. I gave those up. I used to pack shampoo and the like, too. These days I hardly carry any toiletries; I buy them wherever I am.

## What stays behind

This might be one of the biggest problems. Every flat is missing something. I can't always take what I buy with me, so I end up leaving it behind.

Sometimes not having a spatula is enough to make daily life a lot worse. I wish hosts paid a little more attention to things like that.

## What I do now

**A small suitcase, within the limits.** If it doesn't fit, it doesn't come.

**A cheap one.** When it breaks, a new one.

**Digital over physical.** What I can use anywhere doesn't take up space.

A bigger bag would carry more. But a smaller one makes me ask a better question: do I really need this?

I wish you a suitcase that's easy to carry, and a life that doesn't need a bigger one.
