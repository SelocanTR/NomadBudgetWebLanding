Does a nomad need a home?

A lot of people say no. Sell everything. Close everything. Keep only what fits in a bag.

I kept mine.

Having a home I can always go back to makes me feel at ease. So I don't close it, and I keep paying some of its bills.

## What a home gives me

### A safety net

The road is exciting. It's also uncertain. A flat falls through, a plan changes, something happens back home.

Absolutely. Especially during the pandemic. Travel restrictions on the routes I had planned kept growing, I had problems with clients, and my options shrank. My home saved me then.

If I had closed it completely, I would still have found other solutions. But always having a safety net brings a different kind of ease.

### A place for everything else

Because my home is still there, I don't have to carry everything with me. Sometimes I go back home and set off for the next route from there. That way I can leave behind what I don't want and take what I do.

### Somewhere that stays the same

Every one, three or six months, everything around me changes: the flat, the language, the supermarket, the way home.

Sometimes this constant relearning wears you down. Like anyone, I want to go back to my comfort zone for a while.

Somewhere I can't say what I want in a language I don't know. Or I can say it, but cultural differences mean I'm not quite understood. Coming back from that to somewhere deeply familiar is a real relief.

## What it asks of you

A home you don't live in still costs money. Some bills keep running every month, wherever you are.

I keep those costs in their own place. In Nomad Budget, everything I spend in Turkey still goes into my Turkey wallet. That way the country I'm living in and the home I left behind show up separately, and together in the total.

For me, it's worth it. It's a cost I pay on purpose.

## Coming back

I don't have a fixed rule or schedule. It depends entirely on how I feel and how much it costs.

In my first years on the road, I came back more often, every three months or so. Then that stretched to six months, then a year. With intercontinental trips, it can be longer than a year.

The moment I walk through the door, I know what I'll find. A rental is never like that. You book it from far away and walk into the unknown. Fake reviews, flats that look clean but aren't quite, flats that look comfortable but aren't quite: they wear you down a little. My own home isn't like that.

When I'm away, I miss home a little. When I'm home, I miss travelling. An endless loop that never changes.

## What "home" means now

Honestly, I'm not sure either. I spent my childhood in one place, so that place felt like home. Now that I never stay in one place, nowhere gives me that feeling. The places change, and so do the people.

Anywhere I feel happy can be home now.

I wish you a place to come back to, whatever shape it takes.
