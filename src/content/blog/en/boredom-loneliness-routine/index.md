Have you ever felt stuck?

I have. Sometimes at work, sometimes in other parts of life. Most of the time I didn't even look for a way out. I threw myself into more work. Not thinking about what had me stuck felt like the only escape.

Even as a designer, in work that needed me to be creative, it was the same. I was a hamster in its wheel: running all the time, and always in the same place.

Years ago I wrote about the way out in [Boredom is The Only Solution When You Feel Stuck](https://selcuksevindik.medium.com/boredom-is-the-only-solution-when-you-feel-stuck-f05bbc5618e2).

I needed to get bored.

From the outside, nomad life looks like the opposite of boredom. New countries, new cities, new people. From the inside, there are three quiet struggles. None of them shows up in the photos.

## Three quiet struggles

### Staying in

In Buffer's 2023 survey of 3,000 remote workers, the most common struggle wasn't loneliness. 33% said "I stay home too often because I don't have a reason to leave," and 21% picked it as their biggest struggle.

I know this one well. Working from home all the time kills my motivation — I need change. No office means no reason to go out. You have to create that reason yourself.

### Loneliness

In the same survey, loneliness came second: 23% chose it, 15% called it their biggest struggle.

As a nomad, you add one more thing: every move, your circle starts over. The people you've just got used to end up on the other side of a screen when you leave.

Traveling with someone helps. A lot. But it doesn't fill every gap.

I still miss the people I love.

The distance isn't the hardest part. The moments are. Someone at home needs help, and all you can do is call. A birthday, a special day, and you're there only through a screen.

And the new people? You meet plenty of them. Keeping the conversation going is the hard part.

Where are you from? How long are you staying? Where next?

Most of it stays small talk.

### Routine

The joy of travel is novelty. Work wants routine.

Every move breaks it: a new flat, a new desk, new internet, a new supermarket. On short stays, you're back on the road before a routine can form.

That's one nice side effect of choosing stays of one, three or six months: they make room for routine. Stay long enough, and a city stops being a place you visit and becomes a place you live.

## Getting bored works

On the road, there's little room to get bored. There's always something to do, see, plan.

But the brain needs a break sometimes. Constant novelty, constant busyness: it spins the wheel faster, and doesn't move it an inch.

Getting bored is stopping the wheel. Sometimes the best ideas come when there's nothing left to do.

I don't need to be doing something every moment.

I go for walks without headphones. No podcast, no music. Just me and my thoughts.

Or I drink my coffee and watch the sunset. That's it.

You don't need a plan for it. You just need some time to think.

## Motivation has a budget

Let's not use my own numbers. Let's use a round example. Prices depend on the city and the habit; the point is to see the size of it.

You work 22 days a month. Working from home costs **0 units**. But motivation drops.

- **A café every day:** coffee and something small, 4 units a day. 22 × 4 = **88 units**
- **A coworking membership:** **120 units** a month
- **Café three days a week, home two:** 13 days × 4 = **52 units**

None of these is "wasted money." It's the price of a reason to go out. What matters is seeing the line, and choosing it on purpose.

## What I do now

**I create a reason every day.** I keep trying new cafés, looking for quiet places with comfortable seats. For me a café isn't a coffee. It's a kind of office.

**I stay long enough for a routine.** I choose stays of one, three or six months over short ones.

**I keep the café line separate.** In Nomad Budget, café spending has its own category. When café money disappears into "eating out," I can't see what it adds up to. Seeing it on its own, I can also see what it invests in my motivation.

The wheel still starts spinning sometimes. But now I can notice it. That's the first step to stopping.

I wish you a little boredom on the road, now and then.
