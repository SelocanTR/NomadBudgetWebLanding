Where do your best ideas come from?

Mine rarely come at the desk.

I'm a designer. For years I was a hamster in its wheel: running all the time, and always in the same place. I wrote about it in [my post on boredom, loneliness and routine](/blog/boredom-loneliness-routine/).

Travel changed a lot of that. In a way I never expected.

I didn't think travel would have any effect on my creativity. It has had an incredible one. The more cultures and the more work I see, the more easily I make connections in every new project. I believe there's a huge difference between a designer who never leaves their room and a designer who travels.

An ad I saw on the metro, a modern art gallery I walked through: every idea I collect becomes part of a blend in my head. Everything the people I meet tell me plants new seeds, and they sprout. You find yourself in an amazing creative flow.

## New places shake things up

Every new country is a step out of the comfort zone. New rules, new people, a different order of things. Years ago I wrote about how shy that makes me in [I Feel Shy Every Time I Arrive in a New Country](https://selcuksevindik.medium.com/i-feel-shy-every-time-i-arrive-in-a-new-country-f6f6e67f57ec).

That discomfort is useful. When nothing is familiar, you notice everything.

Not one place. Every place does. Both what I see and what I learn from other people.

As a result, my work has become more practical and easier to adapt, and I can connect local cultural values to a context that goes beyond any one country.

## But novelty alone isn't enough

On the road, there's always something to do, see, plan. Constant novelty spins the wheel faster. It doesn't move it an inch.

Ideas need the opposite too: quiet.

I don't need to be doing something every moment. I go for walks without headphones, just me and my thoughts. Or I drink my coffee and watch the sunset. You just need some time to think.

## Change as a tool

Working from home all the time kills my motivation. So I keep changing places: a new café, a new street, sometimes a new country.

When I'm stuck on a problem, this really works. But I don't just change places; I switch to a different task. After a while, I come back to what I was stuck on. A rested mind with a fresh focus finds a solution much more easily.

## An idea from the road

The hard parts of life on the road, the things I've seen, the way people work and build their businesses, the wish to make work that's about quality, and passion. All of it lines up with what I want to do with Nomad Budget.

That's what Nomad Budget is for: to make things easier for people who want to take this road, and to spare them some of the struggles I went through.

## What I do now

**I leave room to get bored.** Not every hour needs a plan.

**I keep changing places.** New cafés, new streets. Change is part of how I work.

**When I'm stuck, I switch tasks.** When I come back, the answer comes more easily.

**I walk without headphones.** Just me and my thoughts.

New places bring new ideas. But they only land when there's enough quiet to hear them.

I wish you new places, and enough quiet to hear the ideas they bring.
