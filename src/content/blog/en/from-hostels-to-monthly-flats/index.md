Do you remember your first big trip?

I do.

Vienna, Budapest, Kraków, Prague. Hostels. A new city every couple of days. A 65-litre backpack I'd researched so much, I could have called myself a backpacking expert.

I think everyone should try hostel life at least once while they're young. You meet people easily, and they're people who are there to travel, just like you. It's also much cheaper.

What felt strangest was people coming in and out of the room all the time. I wasn't used to it. I was a light sleeper, and I had to get used to that.

Since 2020 I've lived on the road. Almost everything about the way I travel has changed since that first trip.

## Then and now

**Moving.** Then: every two days. Now: every one, three or six months. I wrote about why in [my post on slow travel](/blog/slow-travel-one-three-six-months/).

**The bag.** Then: a 65-litre backpack. Now: a wheeled suitcase. Not a big one; I always keep it within the limits. I said goodbye to the backpack in [No More Backpacking, I Love My Wheeled Suitcase](https://medium.com/millennials/no-more-backpacking-i-love-my-wheeled-suitcase-6df6d435e40b).

**The bed.** Then: a hostel dorm. Now: a flat with its own kitchen, rented by the month. I explained what that costs in [my post on the short-term rent premium](/blog/short-term-rent-premium/).

**The money.** Then: I didn't track anything. At the end of the trip I paid a card statement in euros, and by then the euro had gone up. Now: every expense is recorded the same day. It's a habit.

## Why it changed

A short trip and a long stay are very different things. One, three or six months don't fit in a backpack. And work wants routine; a new city every two days doesn't leave room for one.

There was no "no more hostels" moment. It was a slow shift. First it was only hostels. Then a mix of hostels and hotels. Then hotels and rented flats.

As I got a bit older, I wanted quieter sleep and some privacy. And since I could afford it, I made the switch.

## What I miss

I still stay in a hostel now and then, though rarely. It's so easy to connect with people there, and I really love that. Almost everyone is open-minded and happy to share.

## What stayed the same

**I still research before I go.** Back then it was the backpack. Now it's the way from the airport to the flat.

**I still look for new places.** Then it was a new city every two days. Now it's a new café, a new street, a new neighbourhood, within the same city.

The traveller who changed cities every two days and the one who stays for months are the same person. One of them just knows a little more about what works.

I wish you a way of travelling that fits the life you want to live.
