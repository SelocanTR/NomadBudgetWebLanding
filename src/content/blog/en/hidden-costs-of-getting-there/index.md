How much did your last flight really cost?

Before my first trip I did so much research I could have called myself a backpacking expert. I bought a very comfortable 65-litre backpack. Everything I needed fit in it, and with small liquids I could even take it on the plane. No waiting for bags at the airport.

Years later I wrote about saying goodbye to that backpack in [No More Backpacking, I Love My Wheeled Suitcase](https://medium.com/millennials/no-more-backpacking-i-love-my-wheeled-suitcase-6df6d435e40b). A short trip and a long stay are very different things. One, three or six months don't fit in a backpack. I switched to a suitcase.

Everything got easier.

It's not a big suitcase. I always keep it within the limits.

Things stay organised. Nothing hangs on your shoulders. Rain? Dirt? It's just plastic. Wipe it and go.

And I don't buy expensive ones. I let it break, then buy a new one and move on.

The suitcase took the weight off my shoulders. The price of the journey is still more than the ticket, though.

Looking at the ticket and thinking "cheap" isn't enough.

## The ticket isn't the trip

### Bags

In 2014 the EU Court of Justice drew a clear line: hand luggage of reasonable weight and size is an indispensable part of carrying a passenger, so it can't be charged extra. Checked baggage isn't part of the essential service; airlines can charge for it separately.

Where "reasonable" ends is still being fought over. In November 2024, Spain's consumer affairs ministry fined five low-cost airlines a total of €179 million. The reasons included charging extra for cabin bags, charging for adjacent seats for people travelling with children or dependants, disproportionate fees for printing boarding passes, and unclear prices. The airlines said they would appeal.

So the rules change by country, by airline and by year. One thing doesn't change: the first price you add to the basket isn't the price you'll pay.

### Seats and small fees

Seat selection. Priority boarding. A boarding pass printed at the airport. Each one small. Together, close to the ticket itself.

### The airport

Cheap flights often use airports far from the city. That's two more rides: to the airport, and from it. Land at midnight, and the ride becomes a taxi. I wrote about those first-day costs in [my post on the first week in a new country](/blog/first-week-in-a-new-country/).

## One journey in numbers

Let's not use my own numbers. Let's use a round example. Fees depend on the airline, the route and the date; the point is to see the size of it.

The flight on your screen costs **40 units**. For someone moving for one, three or six months, the real journey is:

- A cabin bag: **+25 units**
- A checked suitcase: **+35 units**
- Seat selection: **+10 units**
- Getting to the airport, and from the airport to the flat: 2 × 15 = **+30 units**

A 40-unit ticket, a **140-unit** journey. Three and a half times the ticket.

A 90-unit train or bus ticket on the same route looks expensive at first. If it includes your bags and goes from city centre to city centre, it's cheaper than the flight.

## What I do now

**I compare journeys, not tickets.** And for me, a journey isn't just the ticket and the bags. It's everything I spend to live there. Getting there is one line in the cost of living.

**I move rarely, and stay long.** I prefer stays of one, three or six months to short ones. There's a nice side effect: every move is a cost, bags and transfers included, and moving less shares that cost across more months.

**I don't force myself.** When I have the chance and the options, I avoid very late and very early flights. Of course, it's a matter of budget. And sometimes I choose a land route instead of a flight.

**I write the journey down where I am.** In Nomad Budget, I usually add the cost of a move to the wallet of the country I'm in at that moment. What matters is that it's written down. A journey left off the books makes a move look cheaper than it was.

That 65-litre backpack is still a good memory. But today, when I look at the price of a trip, I write the backpack, the suitcase and the ride from the airport next to it.

I wish you journeys as cheap as their tickets.
