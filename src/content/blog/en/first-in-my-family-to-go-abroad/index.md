Who was the first person in your family to go abroad?

In mine, it was me.

I grew up in Adana, the child of a working family. No one before me had travelled abroad.

As a child, "abroad" wasn't really a thing in our lives. Only when relatives or neighbours who lived in Germany came to visit, they'd bring chocolate, and we'd be thrilled: we were eating German chocolate. Back then, abroad looked completely out of reach.

My first trip abroad was in 2013. I had saved for it for a long time. Even getting my first passport was exciting, and just as scary.

I went alone for two weeks, starting in Vienna and visiting a few countries. I stayed in hostels and met new people.

I felt like I'd done something most people wouldn't even dare to do. In fact, I'd just got on a plane and gone to a neighbourhood where other people live. It wasn't such a big deal.

Back then I was already working as a designer in corporate jobs. Then, in 2020, I started a life on the road as a digital nomad.

## How the family saw it

On my first trip abroad, they were as excited as I was. They had never seen anywhere abroad either. Worry, pride, confusion: they felt all three at once.

Living on the road is an unusual choice for them. But they've always supported me. They're so curious about what I'm living that they ask about every detail.

## Being the one who is far away

I still miss the people I love.

The distance isn't the hardest part. The moments are. Someone at home needs help, and all you can do is call. A birthday, a special day, and you're there only through a screen.

Every now and then I go back to my own home, and on those trips I visit them too and make up for the time apart. Otherwise, we talk on the phone often and send each other photos.

## What it changed

Being the first means there was no one to ask. No one who had done it before, no one to tell you what to pack or what to expect.

My family feels as if they've travelled along with me. They're learning things they never knew about places all over the world.

## A home to come back to

I kept my home. Having a place I can always go back to makes me feel at ease. I wrote about it in [A Home to Come Back To](/blog/a-home-to-come-back-to/).

## What I wish someone had told me

If you're going to be the first in your family, let me tell you what nobody told me back then.

It's not as big a deal as it feels in your head. There's nothing to be afraid of, nothing to worry about. It's much easier than you think.

And it's easier now than it was years ago. You can learn about almost everything before you go. Trust in people's good intentions, but stay alert so you don't get scammed.

I wish you an easy first step, and people waiting for you at home.
